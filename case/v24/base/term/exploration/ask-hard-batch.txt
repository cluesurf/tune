You are naming living things for a constructed language whose
words are built from about 4000 plain concepts. A name must be a
literal compound of ordinary concrete words.

For each species you are given, do four things in order.

1 DESCRIBE it richly. What does it look like: shape, colour, size,
  texture, the leaf, the flower, the seed. Where does it live:
  ground, water, rock, tree, cold, hot. What does it do: climb,
  creep, float, sting, cling, open at night. How does it feel to
  the senses: smell, taste, touch, sound in wind. What do people
  use it for: food, medicine, dye, rope, timber. What would a
  person notice FIRST on meeting it.

2 GATHER the traits from that description that could name it.

3 DISTIL to the two or three most telling. Ranked by: minimalism,
  fewest parts; accuracy, it must be true of this species and not
  its neighbours; obviousness, a speaker should guess it.

4 NAME it as two or three plain English concepts joined by +.

Rules. Never use a proper name, a person, a place, or a people.
Never use a technical botanical term: say leaf cup, not calyx.
Use only plain concrete words a hunter gatherer would know, plus
ordinary modern ones. If you genuinely do not know the species,
say so in `why` and leave `name` empty rather than inventing.
