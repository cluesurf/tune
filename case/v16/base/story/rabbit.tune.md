# Rabbit, in Tune

A gloss, not a text. **The 51 closed words and every content root are
written in English**, because neither has been assigned a form yet, so
what is being tested here is the STRUCTURE.

Written 2026-09-20 against `note/tune/grammar/reference.md`. It
replaces an older translation made in the v0 lexicon and a different
grammar, and shares nothing with it.

```text
-i  action        -a  object        -o  feature, as head
-e  relation      bare  modifier    +   inside one compound word
```

**Read the English line, then the Tune line under it.** Where a
paragraph needed a decision the grammar had not made, a note follows
it, and every one of those notes is collected at the foot of the page.
Punctuation is for a reader of English and is not part of Tune.

## One

> "Mmmm..." I stretch, opening my eyes. My legs are still quietly
> tucked in. Mmm... My home feels extra cozy today. Don't want to wake
> up, let those eyes droop down for just a little longer! Mmmm!! The
> burrow has an unusually musty smell about it. It is time to do work!
> Chipper me this. And all will be well, just wait and see.

```text
mmm.
I-a stretch-i, open-i my eye-a.
my leg-a continue quiet tuck-o.
mmm.
my home-a very warm-o at-e this day-a.
not want-i wake-i. command let-i my eye-a fall-i to-e small more time-a.
mmm.
burrow-a have-i strange old smell-a.
this-a work time-a.
command I-a bright-o.
and-e all-a future good-o. command wait-i and-e see-i.
```

**`my eye-a` is my eye.** A possessive is one word and it is a
modifier, so it is bare like any modifier. See note one.

## Two

> Hop hop hop, out of my burrow. Emerging from the ground, my hind
> legs support my peeping eyes. Look around! See the beauty. Witness
> the infinite unfolding. The rays of the sun warmly touch me like a
> kiss.

```text
hop-i hop-i hop-i, from-e my burrow-a.
rise-i from-e ground-a, my back leg-a hold-i my small+open eye-a.
command around see-i. command see-i beauty-a.
command see-i not+end open-a.
sun light-a same warm touch-i I-a from-e kiss-a.
```

**`same warm touch-i ... from-e kiss-a` is the simile.** It is the
equative, `same tall-o from-e Mary-a`, as tall as Mary, put on the
verb: touches me as warmly as a kiss would. `same` modifies, and the
standard sits on `from` with the other relations. **`same` is not a
relation**, and a first draft of this page wrote `same-e kiss-a` nine
times before the checker learned to refuse it. See note seven.

## Three

> Surrounded by green and purple, red and blue, I feel the yellow
> chanting with all of us. Sniff. Sniff. "What's this?" Perching on
> all toes, I freeze. Ears kept sharp and steady. Bloop. A bit of rain
> settling into the dirt. Wow, so cool.

```text
green-a and-e purple-a and-e red-a and-e blue-a around-i I-a,
  I-a feel-i yellow-a sing-i with-e all we-a.
sniff-i. sniff-i.
this-a what-a?
I-a stand-i at-e all toe-a, and-e stop-i. my ear-a sharp-o and-e steady-o.

```

**`this-a what-a?` is the content question**, with `what` sitting
where the answer goes and nothing fronted.

## Four

> A soft breeze glances my fur. Boom, it's wonderful to be alive. To
> be tantalized by the aroma of wild sage; to be seated amongst the
> tallest pines. Pluck! A blackberry. What more could one want.

```text

bum. live-i very good-o.
smell-i wild sage-a good-o. sit-i at-e most tall pine-a good-o.
```

**"It is wonderful to be alive" is an action as subject**, which the
grammar allows without comment: `live-i` heads its own clause and
`very good-o` describes it.

## Seven

> Now it's time for work. Things to be done. Ideas to create. Problems
> to illustrate, solutions to spiral back around, like the tunnels we
> so generously have dug. More must be dug. Gems uncovered. Roots
> nibbled upon, areas explored and safety and benevolence experienced.
> After all, this process needs some company. Let the mind mould the
> earth, and I should have a safe restful home for the night. Sniff
> Sniff.

```text
this-a work time-a.
thing-a must do-i. idea-a must make-i. problem-a must show-i.
answer-a must same back turn-i from-e we-a past dig-i tunnel-a.
more tunnel-a must dig-i. stone+light-a must find-i.
root-a must eat-i. place-a must see-i.
safe-a and-e good+heart-a must feel-i.
this way-a want-i some friend-a.
command mind-a shape-i earth-a,
  and-e I-a must have-i safe rest home-a at-e night-a.
sniff-i. sniff-i.
```

**`thing-a must do-i` is the whole passive.** There is no voice, so a
thing in the first slot with no doer named simply leaves the doer
unsaid. English needs "to be done" and Tune needs nothing.

**"After all" is dropped.** See note four.

## Thirteen

> Rain. We must master the rain. Know how it starts, and how to bring
> it back when things are near. We must find the patterns in the
> light and night, in the big and small, in the rough and smooth,
> until it just clicks.

```text
rain-a.
we-a must master-i rain-a.
we-a must know-i it-a start-i with-e what+way-a,
  and-e know-i we-a back bring-i it-a with-e what+way-a at-e thing-a close-o.
we-a must find-i pattern-a at-e light-a and-e night-a,
  at-e big-a and-e small-a, at-e rough-a and-e smooth-a,
  to-e we-a complete know-i it-a.
```

**`rain-a.` alone is a name**, and it opens the paragraph the way the
English does. See note two.

**`with-e what+way-a` is "how"**, by what means. `what+way` is a
compound and a compound is a thing, so it takes `-a` and the relation
in front of it says which role it plays. `start-i` and `close-o` are
content roots: `begin` and `near` are closed words, aspect markers,
and in a gloss they would collide with the English verb and adjective.

**`this-a work time-a` in paragraph one is "it is time for work".**
`this` is a demonstrative and a demonstrative can stand as a thing
with `-a`, this one, and then two objects in a row identify. See note
six.

# What the story found

Twenty six paragraphs, and **seven things the sentence tests never
reached.** Each is a discourse fact rather than a clause fact, which is
what a story was expected to expose. Three changed the grammar. Three
were already answered and are recorded so the next reader does not
re-derive them. One was a hole in the checker that let this page be
wrong nine times.

**Every Tune line on this page was run through `check.ts`** after the
changes below, 179 utterances, and none is refused. The onomatopoeia
is skipped, because it is not words. `pnpm check:tune:story` from
`mesh/` runs it again.

## One. The possessives are five words

`my` appears eleven times in this story and `our` seven more. Writing
each as `home-a of-e I-a` is three words for the commonest thing
anybody says about a thing, and **the most said words should be the
shortest**, which is the same argument that made `we` and `they` words
on 2026-09-20.

```text
my    your    its    our    their
```

**One per pronoun, and they are modifiers**, so they are bare like any
modifier and take `-o` when they stand as the head:

```text
my home-a           my home
its four part-a     its four phases
book-a my-o         the book is mine
```

**Adopted.** The closed list is 51. `of-e` still does every other
genitive: `hole-a of-e this stone-a`, `path-a of-e wild place-a`.

## Two. A term alone is an utterance

The story is full of these.

```text
Rain.                   rain-a.
A few crickets.         few sing+bug-a.
My home.                my home-a.
Ah, my friend!          my friend-a!
```

**The checker used to refuse every one** with "nothing is said",
because it was written to check a CLAUSE and these are not clauses.

They are not errors. A fragment is how every language names a thing,
answers a question, calls somebody, and writes a heading. A grammar
that refuses them cannot write a title.

**Adopted.** A term alone is a well formed utterance: it names rather
than asserts, which is a different speech act and not a broken
sentence. The vocative comes free with it, and so does the answer to a
question. `check` now accepts a lone term and `checkClause` is the
stricter gate for when an assertion is wanted.

## Three. "We are here" is `exist-i`

There is no copula, and "we are here" has no feature and no action to
carry it. **The verb of being somewhere is `exist-i`**, and the place
is a relation phrase after it, the way it is after any verb.

```text
we-a exist-i at-e this place-a     we are here
you-a exist-i at-e that place-a    there you are
```

This is what Mandarin does with 在 and it was already how the stress
test wrote "there is a cloud". Nothing changed. It is recorded because
the first draft of this page wrote `we-a at-e this place-a`, which is
a name and not a claim.

## Four. The discourse connectives

A story hangs together with words a sentence never needs.

| English | Tune | how |
| --- | --- | --- |
| but, and yet | `and-e` | the contrast is in what is said, decided before this story |
| finally | `at-e end-a` | a relation phrase set first |
| now, for now | `at-e this time-a` | the same |
| when | `at-e` and a clause | the reach rule |
| after all | dropped | see below |

**"After all" is the one that had no home.** It marks a justification
the listener is assumed to already accept, which is a cause plus a
stance toward the listener. `from-e` carries the cause and nothing
carries the stance. It was dropped from paragraph seven and the
sentence lost nothing a reader would notice. **Not adopted**: one
connective is not worth a fifty second word, and the stance it carries
is the beginning of the speech level system that was refused.

## Five. Onomatopoeia is written as heard

```text
mmm    blop    bum    bam    wu wu wu
```

`sniff` is a verb and translates cleanly. **The rest are not words at
all** and were written through as heard. None is a legal root: each
breaks the shape rules on purpose, which is what makes it sound like
the thing.

**Adopted as a stated exemption.** Onomatopoeia sits outside the root
shape rules, the way it does in every language, and the checker does
not read it. The 25 interjections set aside on 2026-09-19 are a
separate matter and are still set aside.

## Six. A modifier word can stand as a thing

The checker refused the first paragraph's `this-a work time-a`, "it is
time for work", and `all-a future good-o`, "all will be well". It had
let a modifier word head only as a feature, `dog-a big-o`, and here
`this` and `all` head as THINGS: this one, everything.

**Every natural language lets these words stand alone.** English says
"this is it", "all is well", "what more". Mandarin's 这 is a pronoun
and a determiner in one word.

```text
this-a work time-a        this is work time
all-a future good-o       all will be well
what more-a               what more
future-a                  the future
question-a                a question
```

**Adopted.** A modifier word heads as a feature with `-o` or as a thing
with `-a`, and takes the ending of what it is. That is rule 3, anything
derivable is derived, and it means `everything`, `the past`, `the
future`, `a question` and `a command` are none of them roots. The
checker's separate rule that a scoping word cannot be last was removed,
because the bare rule already says it and the new endings are heads.

## Seven. `same` is not a relation, and the checker had not been told

The first draft of this page wrote the simile as `same-e kiss-a`, nine
times, and the checker passed every one. It had no entry for the
`operator` group, so `same` could take any ending at all. Neither had
it entries for `and`, `not` and `what`.

**The reference is clear and the page was not reading it.** The
equative is `John-a same tall-o from-e Mary-a`, as tall as Mary: `same`
modifies and the standard sits on `from`. There are seven relations,
they are Pāṇini's, and `same` is not one of them.

```text
same warm touch-i I-a from-e kiss-a       touches me as warmly as a kiss
same ready-o from-e wind-a                as ready as the wind
same-o from-e new born child-a            the same as a newborn
```

**Fixed in the checker**, which now holds all fourteen groups to their
endings, and fixed on this page. The lesson is the one every checker
teaches: a rule the checker does not hold is a rule the text will
break.

## What did NOT break

Worth saying, because the list above is what a reader remembers.

**Every clause in twenty six paragraphs translated with the rules as
written**, and the three changes above are all to the closed list and
the endings a closed word may take, not to the five rules. No missing
relation, no missing aspect.

The things that carried the most weight:

- **`at` takes a clause**, which gave every "when" in the story
  without a word for when
- **the equative on the verb**, `same touch-i ... from-e kiss-a`, gave
  every simile, and there are nine
- **the relative clause with no pronoun** handled "the problem we're
  facing", "the mystery I've unravelled" and "the unseen" without
  comment
- **no voice** handled "things to be done", "gems uncovered", "roots
  nibbled upon" by simply not naming a doer
- **`command`** handled fourteen imperatives, including the ones
  English writes without a subject
- **a bare root modifies the action** as easily as a thing, so `back
  turn-i`, `in breathe-i`, `around see-i` needed no phrasal verbs
